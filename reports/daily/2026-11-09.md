### [FINANCIAL & BANKING · INSTITUTIONAL] — Nvidia launches probe into reports of foreign intelligence effort targeting CEO

**What happened:**

American multinational technology company, Nvidia is opening an investigation into reports that CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The company’s board of directors made the announcement after Huang acknowledged to the board that he had been intimate with a business executive Alice Huanga, a Chinese citizen and chip buyer, whom US intelligence determined was gathering information for Beijing. Huang apologised in a message to the company, saying he was unfaithful to his wife and his actions hurt her and their children for which he is deeply sorry. Nvidia’s board said in a statement it has no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, but that its inquiry would examine all available information. Huang fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it, the board said. Last month, US intelligence agencies had discovered that Huang had developed a close relationship with Alice Huang and determined that she was feeding information directly to Beijing.

### [EDUCATION · REGULATION] — Centre sets up panel to review UGC’s 2026 equity regulations

**What happened:**

The Centre has told the Supreme Court that it is setting up a special committee to review the UGC’s 2026 equity regulations for higher education institutions. The court has stressed that the interests of all students must be protected. Promoting equity in higher education institutions means ensuring that all students get fair treatment, equal opportunities and protection from discrimination in colleges and universities, regardless of their caste or social background. A bench comprising Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi was informed by Solicitor General Tushar Mehta that the government was examining measures to address students’ concerns and ensure no section feels excluded or threatened. The court adjourned the batch of petitions for four weeks, stressing that the welfare and well-being of the younger generation must remain central to the deliberations. The petitions were filed in 2019 by the mothers of Rohith Vemula and Payal Tadvi, who died by suicide after allegedly facing campus caste discrimination, seeking stronger safeguards in higher education institutions. The UGC notified the 2026 Regulations in January, replacing the 2012 framework. The Supreme Court later stayed them, citing concerns over vague provisions and possible misuse, and restored the 2012 rules in the interim.
