### [FINANCIAL & BANKING · REGULATION] — Directions under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 – Samarth Urban Co-operative Bank Ltd., Osmanabad (Maharashtra), – Extension of Period

**What happened:**

The Reserve Bank of India issued Directions under Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 to Samarth Urban Co-operative Bank Ltd., Osmanabad, Maharashtra, vide Directive No. NGP.DOS.SSM1.No.S581/15-04-381/2025-2026 dated October 06, 2025, for a period of six months up to the close of business on April 07, 2026, which were last extended up to the close of business on October 07, 2026, vide Directive No. DOR.RG/D-24/15-04-381/2026-2027 dated July 02, 2026. The Reserve Bank of India is satisfied that in the public interest, it is necessary to further extend the period of operation of the Directive beyond close of business on October 07, 2026. Accordingly, the Reserve Bank of India, in the exercise of powers vested in it under sub-section (1) of Section 35A read with Section 56 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, hereby extends the Directive for a further period of three months from close of business on October 07, 2026, to close of business on January 07, 2027, subject to review. The aforesaid extension and /or modification by the Reserve Bank of India should not per se be construed to imply that the Reserve Bank of India is satisfied with the financial position of the bank.

**Why it matters:**

This matters to banks because it may change prudential, conduct, disclosure, onboarding or customer-protection duties in financial services. Teams should check scope, commencement and transition provisions against the source document. Implementation owners should map the instrument against existing policies, products and controls.

### [MACROECONOMY, TRADE & PUBLIC FINANCE · DATA] — India’s foreign exchange reserves decline by $18.3 billion to $747.5 billion

**What happened:**

The country’s foreign exchange reserves declined by 18.3 billion US dollars (USD) to 747.5 billion US dollars during the week ended the 25th of September. According to Reserve Bank of India data, Foreign currency assets, the largest component of the reserves, dropped by 15.5 billion USD to 615.4 billion USD during the week. Gold reserves dropped by 2.5 billion USD to 108.7 billion US dollars. Special Drawing Rights fell by 97 million USD to 18.64 billion USD. The country’s reserve position with the International Monetary Fund dipped by 86 million to 4.8 billion USD.

**Why it matters:**

This matters to businesses, investors and public-policy teams because it changes the macroeconomic baseline used by businesses, investors and public-policy teams for demand, financing, trade and fiscal planning. Teams should compare the release with prior data, revisions and their own operating assumptions before changing plans. Implementation owners should map the instrument against existing policies, products and controls.
