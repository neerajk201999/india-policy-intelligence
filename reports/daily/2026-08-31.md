### [DEREGULATION & EASE OF DOING BUSINESS · REGULATION] — Dept of Consumer Affairs notifies Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026

**What happened:**

The Department of Consumer Affairs has notified the Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026. The Rules provide for Indian Standard Time (IST) as the common time reference across the country for legal, administrative, commercial and other official purposes. The common time reference will support accurate time-stamping of banking and digital payment transactions, coordination of railways, airports and other transport systems, and also the coordination of emergency and other time-sensitive services. The Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution said that the Rules will come into force one hundred eighty days from the date of their publication in the Official Gazette. The rules are aimed at ensuring that important systems across the country use accurate and consistent IST.

This matters to banks, consumers, payment providers, transport operators because systems that timestamp transactions or coordinate time-sensitive services may need to align clocks, logs, audit trails and operating standards with the prescribed IST reference before commencement. ### [MACROECONOMY, TRADE & PUBLIC FINANCE · DATA] — Industrial output growth expanded by 6.7% in July this year on annual basis

**What happened:**

India’s Industrial output growth, as measured by the Index of Industrial Production (IIP), expanded by 6.7 per cent in July this year on an annual basis. The July data indicates that industrial activity continued to expand at a healthy pace, although the pace of growth moderated from the revised June level. The sharp upward revision in June’s IIP growth to 8.8 per cent from 7.3 per cent also indicates that the industrial momentum entering the July quarter was stronger than initially estimated. According to government data, Manufacturing, which carries the heaviest weight in the index, grew by 7.3 per cent, while electricity and gas supply posted an 8.7 per cent expansion in July. Water supply, sewerage and waste management rose by 7.4 per cent. However, the mining sector proved to be a laggard, posting a negative growth of 0.9 per cent last month.

### [MACROECONOMY, TRADE & PUBLIC FINANCE · INSTITUTIONAL] — Petroleum and Natural Gas Ministry clarifies CBG framework under GOBARdhan designed to provide stable price to CBG producers

**What happened:**

The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas has clarified that the revised pricing framework for Compressed Biogas (CBG) under the GOBARdhan Scheme has been designed to provide a stable and viable price to CBG producers while ensuring that consumers do not face any material increase in CNG and household PNG prices. According to a recent newspaper article, the revised price of Compressed Biogas (CBG) under the GOBARdhan Scheme could place a significant additional burden on CNG and household PNG consumers. However, the Ministry explained that this conclusion does not present the correct picture, as it does not account for the Government’s affordability support and the substantially wider domestic gas pool over which the net cost of CBG will now be distributed. The Ministry explained that under the existing system, the procurement price paid to CBG producers is approximately 1 thousand 478 rupees per Million British thermal units, while the revised procurement price under the GOBARdhan Scheme is 2,110 rupees per Million British thermal units. It noted that the Government will also provide affordability support of 10 rupees per kilogram of CBG, equivalent to around 215 rupees per Million British thermal units with 95 per cent methane content, reducing the effective cost to approximately 1,895 rupees per Million British thermal units.
