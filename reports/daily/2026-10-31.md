### [FINANCIAL & BANKING · REGULATION] — SEBI announces relaxation in KYC review rules for FPIs to boost ease of doing business

**What happened:**

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has announced a significant relaxation in the Know Your Customer (KYC) review rules for Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) to boost the ease of doing business. Under the new guidelines issued today, the market regulator has increased the periodicity of KYC reviews for various categories of FPIs. As per the new guidelines, for Category-One FPIs from non-high-risk jurisdictions and certain high-risk entities registered under specific regulations, the KYC review will now be required only once every eight years instead of the earlier shorter timeline. For other FPI categories, including regulated entities and non-high-risk Category-Two investors, the review period has also been rationalised to every eight years, while remaining at every two years for certain remaining entities. In another major relief aimed at reducing the compliance burden, SEBI has completely dispensed with the requirement for FPIs to intimate changes in information or submit a no-change declaration at the time of continuing their registration. FPIs wishing to continue their registration for a subsequent block of three years will now only need to pay the required fees to their Designated Depository Participants (DDP) at least 15 days prior to the expiry of their validity. The regulator said that the KYC review timelines for FPIs investing exclusively in Government Securities and SWAGAT-FI FPIs will remain unchanged.

### [DEREGULATION & EASE OF DOING BUSINESS · INSTITUTIONAL] — US approves $2.1 billion Patriot missile deal for Ukraine; Zelenskyy welcomes approval

**What happened:**

The United States has approved a potential sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 Missile Segment Enhancement (MSE) interceptors to Ukraine at an estimated cost of 2.1 billion US dollars. The acquisition will be funded entirely by European partners and Canada, with no financial contribution from the United States. The US State Department formally announced the approval yesterday, stating that the proposed sale aims to strengthen Ukraine’s air defence capabilities and protect it against current and emerging missile threats. Canada, Germany and Norway are expected to contribute towards the purchase through the US JUMPSTART programme, along with financial support from a European Union credit facility. US defence company Lockheed Martin will serve as the primary contractor for the proposed deal. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has welcomed the US State Department’s approval of the procurement of Patriot missile interceptors to counter incoming Russian ballistic missile attacks. In a social media post, Mr Zelenskyy described the approval as a “strong decision”. He said the 500 PAC-3 interceptors would significantly strengthen Ukraine’s air defence capabilities and could provide an effective defence package during the winter months. However, he urged Washington to expedite their delivery in view of Ukraine’s immediate security needs during the winter season.

### [MACROECONOMY, TRADE & PUBLIC FINANCE · DATA] — Forex reserves decline over $1.2bn last week

**What happened:**

The country’s foreign exchange reserves declined over 1.2 billion US dollars to 719.7 billion US dollars during the week ended on 23rd of this month. According to the Reserve Bank of India’s data, Gold reserves dropped by 2.6 billion US dollars to 103 billion US dollars. Country’s reserve position with the International Monetary Fund fell four million to 4.78 billion US dollars. In contrast, Foreign currency assets, the largest component of the reserves, rose 1.3 billion US dollars to 593.2 billion US dollars during the week. Special Drawing Rights grew by 27 million US dollars to 18.6 billion US dollars.
