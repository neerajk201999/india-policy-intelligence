### [DEREGULATION & EASE OF DOING BUSINESS · INSTITUTIONAL] — FSSAI issues notices to Nestlé India and Hindustan Unilever over misleading food product claims

**What happened:**

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has issued notices to Nestlé India and Hindustan Unilever Limited over misleading claims on the packaging of some of their food products. In a social media post, the food regulator said Hindustan Unilever had made potentially misleading claims on its Horlicks Women’s Plus Chocolate Nutrition Drink and Horlicks Mother’s Plus Kesar products. These include claims relating to bone strength, healthy birth weight and the use of the term “No Added Sugar”. FSSAI said some claims were found to be misleading or inconsistent with applicable food safety regulations. The company has been asked to submit its response within the stipulated period. In a separate case, Nestlé India, along with two dairy manufacturers, has been issued notices over the labelling and online descriptions of its Nescafé ready-to-drink coffee beverages. The regulator flagged inconsistencies in product descriptions and their declared food categories. FSSAI has also issued a notice to Nestlé India over its Resource Renal Vanilla Flavour Powder, intended for the dietary management of people with chronic kidney disease. According to the regulator, the product was marketed without the required prior approval. The companies have been asked to respond to the notices, and further action may follow based on their explanations and the applicable regulations.

### [DEREGULATION & EASE OF DOING BUSINESS · CONSULTATION] — FSSAI draft amendment proposes ban on sucrose and fructose in food products for babies up to six months

**What happened:**

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has published a draft amendment to the Food Safety and Standards (Foods for Infant Nutrition) Regulations, 2020, proposing to prohibit the addition of sucrose and fructose in food products intended for infants up to six months of age. The proposed restriction aims to promote appropriate infant nutrition and support healthy growth and development during the crucial first six months of life. The first six months of life represent a critical period for healthy growth and development, making appropriate nutrition particularly important. The proposed restriction seeks to ensure that infant nutrition products meet the nutritional needs of this age group without the unnecessary addition of sucrose and fructose. The measure is also intended to support broader public health efforts to address concerns associated with early exposure to added sugars, including dietary risk factors linked to childhood obesity and related health challenges. The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has said that the draft notification has been published for public consultation, inviting objections and suggestions from stakeholders within 60 days. The representations received within the prescribed period will be considered by FSSAI before finalisation of the amendment.

### [DEREGULATION & EASE OF DOING BUSINESS · CONSULTATION] — FSSAI notifies draft amendments proposing pesticide maximum residue limits in tea

**What happened:**

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has notified draft amendments proposing Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs) for two pesticides, Acetamiprid and Imidacloprid, in tea. The proposal reflects FSSAI’s approach towards reviewing pesticide residue standards in light of scientific assessments, relevant international practices and the requirements of Indian agriculture. The draft Food Safety and Standards (Contaminants, Toxins and Residues) Amendment Regulations, 2026, propose MRLs of 20 mg per kilogram for Acetamiprid and 50 mg per kilogram for Imidacloprid in tea. The proposed limits are intended to provide clearly defined regulatory benchmarks for pesticide residues, facilitating compliance, monitoring and enforcement of food safety requirements, subject to finalisation of the draft regulations. The proposed standards also recognise the importance of effective pest management in agricultural production. The Ministry highlighted that India’s average pesticide use of less than 0.5 kilogram per hectare remains substantially below the global average of 2.38 kilogram per hectare. It is also lower than the levels reported in several countries, including Colombia, Brazil and the United States of America. Maximum Residue Limits are an important component of food safety regulation. They specify the maximum legally permitted concentration of pesticide residues in food commodities and are established on the basis of scientific assessments, taking into account pesticide residue data, dietary exposure and relevant safety considerations.

### [DEREGULATION & EASE OF DOING BUSINESS · LEGISLATIVE] — Jan Vishwas Act reflects Government’s commitment to Seva and Sushasan

**What happened:**

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has completed 25 years in public service, a journey defined by the mantra of Jan Seva Se Desh Seva, and a relentless commitment that continues. Guided by this vision for Seva and Sushasan, the Government has been ceaselessly working towards achieving the goal of a Viksit Bharat by 2047. Through inclusive development and social empowerment, this dedication is transforming lives. Today, in our special series, we bring you an instance of how this vision has been translated into action through initiatives like the Jan Vishwas Act. For many years, several laws in India treated even small procedural mistakes as criminal offences, and many of these provisions came from older regulatory systems. Recognising the need to make laws more balanced and practical, the Narendra Modi Government began a process of reviewing such provisions. An important step in this direction was the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023, which removed criminal penalties for a number of minor offences across several Central laws. The government continued this reform by introducing the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Bill, 2026, which was passed in Parliament in April this year. When it became an act, it rationalised more than 1000 offences, removing outdated and redundant provisions, thereby improving the overall regulatory environment.

### [FINANCIAL & BANKING · REGULATION] — Centre seeks reports from Punjab govt over killings of anti-drug activists amid narcotics-linked allegations, law and order concerns

**What happened:**

Former Punjab BJP president Sunil Jakhar has written to Union Home Minister Amit Shah, seeking investigations by the National Investigation Agency (NIA) into alleged drug operations being run from prisons in Punjab. Mr Jakhar also demanded a probe by the Enforcement Directorate (ED) into the black money linked to the drug trade in the state. Mr Jakhar said that instead of initiating an NIA investigation or referring the case to the ED, the said drug inspector was reinstated by the Aam Aadmi Party government after his bail. He said that the drug inspector was also appointed to an important position at his department’s headquarters.
